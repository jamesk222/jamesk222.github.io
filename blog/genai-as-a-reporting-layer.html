Using GenAI as a Reporting Layer, Not a Crutch

18 Jun 2026·AI Automation, Reporting

When I say I've integrated ChatGPT and Gemini into reporting workflows, the follow-up question is usually some version of "so the AI writes the report?" Not quite — and the distinction matters more than it sounds.

Where the model sits in the pipeline

The analysis stays mine: the SQL, the DAX, the statistical checks that decide whether a trend is real or noise. What the model does is take a structured summary of what already changed — this week's KPI deltas, flagged anomalies, top movers in a segmentation — and turn it into two or three sentences of plain-English narrative a non-technical stakeholder can read in ten seconds.

That narrowing of scope is deliberate. An LLM given raw data and asked to "summarise the numbers" will happily produce a confident, fluent, occasionally wrong paragraph. An LLM given pre-validated deltas and asked to phrase them clearly is just doing translation — which is a job it's genuinely good at.

The actual workflow

Recurring report runs, computes its metrics and flags anything outside expected range. Only the flagged summary — numbers, not raw rows — gets passed to the API. The response comes back as a short narrative block that sits above the dashboard, and every number in it traces back to a cell I can point to. Nothing gets published unreviewed on the first run of a new report type.

What it actually saved

On recurring analytical reporting, this cut manual write-up time by roughly half — not because the AI does the analysis, but because it removes the twenty minutes I used to spend translating a table into a paragraph a busy stakeholder would actually read. That's the honest pitch for AI in BI: not smarter numbers, just faster, clearer distribution of the numbers you already trust.
